# Phase 10 Step 3 assumptions (A90-A104)

Reserved block A90-A104 (plan: "Reserved id blocks"). Kept in this file, not in `docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md`, so parallel
builders never collide; the supervisor merges. Every row is tagged in the code with its id. All products are
SIMULATED; nothing here describes a real product or agency.

REAL vs ASSUMED. Exactly one family of numbers here is real: the **failure rates**, which are quantiles derived from
public NTD data (`data/derived/fault_rates.yaml`, dataset `ntd_breakdowns_2022_2024`, data.transportation.gov
`amkt-4ehs`): bus miles between major failures, pooled 2022-24, all agencies p10 5,699 / p25 9,607 / p50 18,576 /
p75 47,433 / p90 112,305; small-fleet cut (10-50 vehicles) p10 6,128 / p25 11,840 / p50 26,657 / p75 62,167 /
p90 147,940. The stochastic mode defaults to the small-fleet p50 (26,657 miles) and the quantile is an explicit
parameter. Everything else below is ASSUMED. Call-out and extra-board figures are context from the research digest
(`docs/RESEARCH_DATA_SOURCES.md` sections 4 and 6), not calibration.

| ID | Assumption | Where | Status | Question for the real owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A90 | Ground-truth state model. A vehicle is `ok`; an active fault makes it `faulted`; when the last active fault clears it is `out_of_service` if the crew withdrew it (fault_on param `withdrawn`, default true: a cleared fault still leaves the bus held for inspection) or back to `ok` if not (an intermittent fault the bus runs through); `return_to_service` makes it `ok`. `dvir` and `callout` do not change vehicle state. Truth never depends on any product. The example day is therefore `faulted` 09:40-10:15, `out_of_service` 10:15-11:30, `ok` after. SPN 110 FMI 0 with the stop lamp on is the example fault (the fixture's `pull_vehicle` row) | `world/truth.py`, `world/script.py` | decision | none (synthetic) |
| A91 | Product lag `ProductLag(telematics_s=5, shop_rule_min=20, dispatcher_min=12)`: telematics reports within seconds; a shop rule or foreman acts within 20 minutes (opens the record on a fault or DVIR, closes it after return to service); a dispatcher flips scheduling status, and records a call-out in workforce, within 12 minutes. Round placeholder orders of magnitude, no source. The engine never acts before `truth + lag`; a coarse tick after a long clock jump is late, never early | `world/lag.py`, `world/engine.py` | open | How long do a shop and a dispatch desk really take to key in a breakdown or a call-out? |
| A92 | Actor rules. The shop rule opens a record only for faults that are `service_soon` or `pull_vehicle` by the J1939 fixture's configurable severity rule, or that light the stop lamp, and for every DVIR; it stands down when a record is already open for the vehicle (de-duplication). Records are opened as `human` (a foreman or controller keys it) so the orchestrator reuses one instead of opening a second. The dispatcher pulls a vehicle in scheduling only when the stop lamp is on (the crew parks the bus and calls in), and stands down when scheduling already shows it out of service. Telematics shows only faults that carry an SPN and FMI; any other defect reaches the system only as a driver's DVIR. The shop closes the open record after `return_to_service`; nobody flips scheduling back to `ready`: that stays the orchestrator's approved return step | `world/actors.py` | decision | Do real shops auto-open a record from a fault code, and does the dispatcher or the shop pull the bus in the schedule? |
| A93 | Revenue miles and the service day. There is no default mileage: a caller must pass `revenue_miles_per_day` (a number, or a mapping that names every vehicle); an unknown mileage raises, a known 0.0 means the bus did not run. `ASSUMED_REVENUE_MILES_PER_DAY = 150` is only a placeholder a caller may pass explicitly. The service day is 05:00-21:00 and failure miles are spread evenly across it | `world/stochastic.py` | open | Real revenue miles per vehicle-day and service hours per agency |
| A94 | Which system fails: weights from `config/fault_mix.yaml` (itself ASSUMED, A117/A118, no published split). SPN and FMI: a row of `fixtures/j1939_spn_fmi.yaml` of that system (A115/A116), FMI from the row's `typical_fmi`. A system with no SPN in the fixture (brakes, doors, steering, axles, suspension, air conditioning, body, wheelchair lift/ramp, tyres) is reported by the driver as a DVIR, never by a made-up SPN. Stop lamp = the fixture's severity is `pull_vehicle` | `world/stochastic.py` | decision | Real split of road calls by system; which systems a telematics unit sees |
| A95 | Intermittent faults. 30% of SPN faults clear and recur 1-2 times before the final persistent occurrence; each early occurrence lasts 3-20 min with 10-90 min gaps, and does not withdraw the bus (`withdrawn` false). Shares and durations are invented | `world/stochastic.py` (`P_INTERMITTENT`) | open | How often do real faults clear and recur, and over what time span? |
| A96 | Repair, inspection and downtime. The persistent fault clears after a repair duration drawn lognormal from `config/shop_behaviour.yaml` `repair_hours_by_severity` (A119); the bus returns to service 15-75 min later (inspection). A vehicle that is down accrues no revenue miles, so no new failure is generated for it until it returns; arrivals dropped for that reason are counted in `meta.dropped_while_out_of_service` | `world/stochastic.py` | open | Real repair and sign-off durations |
| A97 | Operator call-out probability 0.07 per operator-shift, sensitivity 0.10 (`CALLOUT_P`, `CALLOUT_P_SENSITIVITY`). Research context, NOT calibration: the sources are agency absenteeism and call-out figures in `docs/RESEARCH_DATA_SOURCES.md` sections 4 and 6 and they describe different things (absence of any kind, different agencies). One shift per operator per day; the call-out lands 30-180 min before the service day starts; extra-board operators are not scheduled and do not call out | `world/stochastic.py` | open | Real call-out rate per operator-shift at the agency |
| A98 | Extra board sized at one or two of every ten operators (`round(n * k / 10)`, k drawn from {1, 2}, at least one operator when there are any); members drawn from the sorted operator ids | `world/stochastic.py` | open | Real extra-board size |
| A99 | Detection latency definitions. Truth = the earliest `fault_on`/`dvir` beat; `truth_to_telematics_s` = telematics' first observation of it; `truth_to_first_proposal_s` = the sim time at which a run first holds a plan (the integrated world stamps each `proposal` as the system goes idle after a step, which happens at the step's own sim time); `truth_to_pull_s` = the sim time of the first successful, non-compensating `set_vehicle_unavailable` write. In the scripted day this includes the human's approval delay (the script approves at 09:41). A stage that did not happen is `null`, never 0. Simulated seconds only | `verification/latency.py`, `integration/world.py::_start_world` | decision | - |
| A100 | `intermittent_fault_clears` is PLAN_VALID with one attached recurrence, not MONITOR. The orchestrator never plans MONITOR for a fault event (the stub reasoner and the code-ranked fallback always pull; MONITOR comes only from a minor PM overdue), so the first occurrence is planned, the recurrence is attached to that run (A9), and the plan is approved. Whether it should monitor an intermittent fault is a product question, not decided here | `verification/scenarios_step3.py` | decision | Should a fault that has already cleared be planned at all? |
| A101 | The telematics mock emits no feed event or webhook when a fault is cleared (`/sim/fault/clear` only drops it from the current-faults list), so the orchestrator cannot see a fault clear unless it polls `/faults/current`. Unchanged from the mock; documented here because the world engine now exercises it | `mocks/telematics.py` (unchanged) | open | Does the real telematics product send a "fault cleared" event? |
| A102 | No mock was changed for Step 3: the engine drives the existing `/sim` and public routes (`/sim/fault`, `/sim/fault/clear`, `/dvir`, `/sim/vehicle-status`, `/workorders`, `/sim/workorders/{id}/close`, `/sim/operator`). A call-out is a `/sim/operator` update that closes the operator's availability window (`available_to` = `available_from`) plus an availability-change row; the export keeps the operator, with a zero-length window | `world/ports.py` | decision | - |
| A103 | Determinism in the stochastic mode. Every random draw comes from `random.Random("<seed>\|<stream>\|<entity id>")` (a string seed), one stream per vehicle, per operator and one for the extra board, so the timeline is byte-identical for a fixed seed, independent of the order ids are listed, and adding a vehicle does not reshuffle the others. `GroundTruth.seed` is recorded for provenance; a hand-written script is fully determined and ignores it | `world/stochastic.py`, `world/truth.py` | decision | - |
